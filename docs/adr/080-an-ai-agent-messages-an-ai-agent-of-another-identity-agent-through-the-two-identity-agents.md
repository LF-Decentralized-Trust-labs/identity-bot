# ADR-080: An AI Agent Messages an AI Agent of Another Identity Agent Through the Two Identity Agents

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-14)
**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Deciders:** Rob Andersen
**Amends:** ADR-047 (AI agents talk through the Identity Agent that holds them): two rules, named in "What this amends"
**Builds on:** ADR-037 (one KERI engine on every platform), ADR-045 (every asset gets an identity and a grant from one schema template), ADR-047, ADR-050 (one check for a controller's actions and an AI agent's capability calls), ADR-067 (a person signs a salted fingerprint of their words), ADR-068 (a conversation's trail keeps who said what, never the words), ADR-075 (a contact is refused on proof of duplicity), ADR-077 (a conversation closes by a signed entry from its opener or its owner)

---

## Context

ADR-047 lets an AI agent ask only AI agents that the same Identity Agent holds. Two Identity Agents that are contacts may each hold AI agents, and those AI agents need to ask each other for work, each side keeping its own systems, without either acting inside the other's. That needs an AI agent to message an AI agent another Identity Agent holds, with each side governing what its own AI agents may send and receive, a record on both sides, and the answer coming back the same way.

Whoever holds each Identity Agent, a person or an organisation, the need and the mechanism are the same. The message, its signatures, its checks and the grant do not differ by holder. Two rules this decision borrows lean on who has standing as the owner, as ADR-047 and ADR-077 define it: who may close a conversation (decision 9), and whether the person who opened a conversation still stands (What this amends, item 1). Where those are settled for one kind of holder and not yet another, the gap is theirs, and decision 9 says what it means here.

Nothing here may be a shared secret or token: each side must be able to show, from its own key log, what it said.

## Decision

1. **The two Identity Agents carry it, as contacts.** A message goes from one Identity Agent to the other inside the envelope the two already use as accepted contacts (an introduction, then an acceptance). It is a new payload type, `agent-peer-message`. Nothing travels from a machine to another Identity Agent directly (ADR-047).

2. **What is signed is a KERI exchange message (`exn`).** The envelope proves which Identity Agent sent the message. The sending Identity Agent also signs an exchange message: KERI's own message for one identity saying something to another, in the exact form keripy builds and a keripy `Exchanger` accepts. Its route names the kind (ask, reply, failure or progress note); its content names the two Identity Agents, the two AI agents, the message's identifier, the ask it answers, and a salted fingerprint of the words (decision 3). It is signed twice: with the Ed25519 messaging key, as KERI attaches a signature, and with the ML-DSA-65 messaging key, as every other message an Identity Agent signs is (no classical-only fallback). The words and the fingerprint's salt travel beside it. The exchange message is a signed message, so each side can keep it and show it to anyone, who checks it against the sending Identity Agent's keys without the other's help. The contract, with keys, signatures and refusals as vectors checked by an independent implementation, is `identity-agent-core/contracts/agent-peer/agent-peer.md` and `identity-agent-core/agentpeer/peer-vectors.json`.

   *Where the exchange message lives.* In keri-go (`exchange.go`), the one KERI engine every platform uses (ADR-037), not in this repository: a KERI message is the engine's to build and read, so a second implementation here would be the second engine ADR-037 removes. This repository calls it, and adds only what is ours: the route, the content, the fingerprint and the post-quantum signature. keri-go's vectors for the message are built by keripy 1.3.6, which also takes each message and refuses each altered copy before it is written down. An earlier draft of this decision carried the same fields in a signed body that only looked like an exchange message; a look-alike of a standard message has to be read for ever beside the real one, so it was not kept.

   *Who signs.* The key-log key of the identity an Identity Agent holds is held by the device that controls it and cannot sign every message the Identity Agent sends. The Identity Agent signs with its messaging keys, which its key log commits to (the DID's `kel_sig`). So the exchange message's sender is the Ed25519 messaging key written as a non-transferable identifier (a key that is its own identifier), which is the one kind of sender keripy checks with nothing else. A receiver accepts it only when that key is the one in the sender's DID it holds for that contact, and the sending Identity Agent's identifier is named in the content. The receiver does not check the key log again for each message, as it does not for any message from a contact; the DID was tied to the key log when the contact was made. A kept message proves that those messaging keys signed it, and a bundle must carry the keys tied to the log (decision 10). Keys a log has since rotated away from are what the log vouched for when the message was made, and showing that later needs the bundle to carry them. A rotation of the messaging keys is a new sender, and a contact whose keys changed is not heard from until its keys are refreshed.

3. **The fingerprint of the words is salted.** The exchange message names the words by the version 1 fingerprint of ADR-067, under a salt made for that message, and names its version, so it cannot be swapped. The salt travels and is kept with the words, never in a signed message or a record. Without it anybody who sees a kept message could confirm a short message ("yes", "approved") by hashing guesses, and spot the same message in two conversations; between AI agents short answers are the common case. Only version 1 is written or read: a new format does not start with the unsalted digest.

4. **What the signatures prove, and what they do not.** They prove that the sending Identity Agent vouched that an AI agent it holds sent these words to the named AI agent of the receiving Identity Agent, at that time. They do **not** prove which machine or brain produced the words, and the receiver cannot check the AI agent's own keys: between AI agents one Identity Agent holds, each AI agent countersigns with its own messaging keys (ADR-047), and another Identity Agent has no way to be sure those keys belong to that AI agent but through the sending Identity Agent's say-so. So **the sending Identity Agent answers for the AI agents it holds**; a receiver that wants more has to ask the other side's owner. This is weaker than ADR-047's countersignature and is chosen as the smaller thing that can be honestly checked.

5. **A message is for one Identity Agent. Not passing one on is the sender's own policy.** The exchange message names its recipient, and one for any other Identity Agent is refused. A message from another Identity Agent is not meant to be passed on to a third, but nothing on the wire can show that an AI agent did not quote what it was told in a new message of its own: words can always be repeated. What is enforced is what each side can enforce. The sender applies its own rule before sending, and the receiver applies its own on receipt. An AI agent that holds an ask from another Identity Agent that it has not yet answered starts no ask to a further one, whether in its own conversation or through another AI agent the same Identity Agent holds. Words can still be carried on after the answer, or by another AI agent it holds that holds no ask; that is not prevented. And a conversation record is a fault when an ask to another Identity Agent's AI agent is recorded inside a conversation that another Identity Agent's AI agent opened. This limits confused-deputy chains and loops between two Identity Agents; it does not make them impossible, and it is not claimed to.

6. **Nothing is taken unless the receiver keeps AI agents and says so.** The core checks that the sender is an accepted contact and that the exchange message holds, and then hands the message to whatever keeps the AI agents this Identity Agent holds and their conversations (an add-on, as the trail is, ADR-068), registered with `CoreServer.OnAgentPeerMessage`. With none, every such message is refused. The core keeps no copy of the words: not as a notification and not with the proof of receipt, so a message the owner's rule refused leaves no plaintext for every controller to read, and a contact cannot grow the core's storage by sending. The keeper applies the receiving owner's rule (decision 7) and what only it can know: that the AI agent is held and live, and how many asks are unanswered.

7. **Each side governs its own, and defaults to nothing.** A new capability, `agent.message.peer`, names the contact, and optionally the AI agent, an AI agent may message (`agentpeer.CheckPeerGrant`: with no constraint naming the contact it allows nothing, and a constraint that is badly shaped allows nothing: a narrowing `agent` that is text or an empty list is a refusal and never "every AI agent"). `agent.message.send` is not widened: with no constraint it means any AI agent the same Identity Agent holds and never another's. Separately each Identity Agent's owner sets, per contact, which of its AI agents may message that contact and which of the contact's they may name, and which of its AI agents that contact may reach, at what rate and with how many asks unanswered. That rule is held and applied by whatever keeps the AI agents, not by the core, and only the owner may set it (an implementation lists its route among those a paired controller may never take, ADR-050). The sender applies its own rule before it calls the core's sending method (`CoreServer.SendAgentPeerMessage`), and the receiver applies its own, so neither trusts the other's governance. The rule governs asks. An answer (a reply, a failure or a progress note) is not held to it: it goes only to the AI agent that put the ask and is taken only as the answer to an ask the taker's own AI agent put, so the ask, let in under the owner's rule in the direction it started, is the permission for its answers.

8. **The receiver tells the sender no more than it must.** A message the receiver refuses is answered with nothing about why (an inbound action's failure is logged, never returned, as for contacts). The reason is kept in the receiver's own record, so a stranger cannot learn its rules by probing.

9. **A conversation is each side's own, and its owner closes a conversation another Identity Agent's AI agent opened.** The receiver opens its own conversation, owned by its Identity Agent and marked as from the other side's AI agent, and hands the ask to its AI agent's body as it would any ask. A reply must name the ask it answers and goes only to that ask's sender; an ask is answered once (ADR-047). Each side's trail keeps the entry with the other's signed exchange message. ADR-077 says a conversation closes by a signed entry from its opener or its owner. The opener of a conversation begun from another Identity Agent is that Identity Agent's AI agent, and its statement has no way to close; so **such a conversation is closed only by this Identity Agent's owner**, and the other side's AI agent cannot close one, reopen one or raise its limit. The owner is the one ADR-077 gives that standing. Today that is an owner the key log names apart from the Identity Agent's own identity; an Identity Agent that is its own owner, as a person's on their own device usually is, has nobody with that standing yet, so such a conversation stays open there until ADR-077 gives the holder a way to close it. Nothing depends on closing it: the cap on conversations open with a contact counts asks that have not been answered, not conversations that nobody can close.

10. **A conversation holds the other Identity Agent's entries as a fourth kind of sender's proof.** An entry from an AI agent another Identity Agent holds is countersigned by this Identity Agent, as a person's is (the AI agent's own keys are not ours to hold), and carries `peer_proof`: the other Identity Agent's signed exchange message, without the words and without the salt. Its sender is written `peer:<Identity Agent>/<AI agent>`, and the countersigned envelope is addressed to that Identity Agent, as `did:keri:<Identity Agent>`; the AI agent is named in the record, which is the body. This Identity Agent's countersignature proves only that it recorded the entry, in that place and at that time, and nothing about the sender. `agentmessage.Record.CheckPeerProof` checks the proof with the other Identity Agent's messaging keys: the exchange message is signed by them, and says what the record says (the message, the two AI agents, the kind, the answer it names, the words' fingerprint). The conversation bundle checks it the same way and requires the bundle to carry that Identity Agent's messaging keys tied to its key log, like any countersigner's; where it carries the words it carries the salt, and the fingerprint is checked. A bundle can show that the other Identity Agent said the AI agent it holds sent the words, and says so in what it cannot rule out. `contracts/agent-message` gains `peer_proof`.

## What this amends

ADR-047 is amended in two places, and carries the matching line.

1. *"The person who opened the conversation must also be allowed to reach that AI agent. An AI agent acting for a person reaches no further than the person."* A person's controller grant has no notion of a contact, so for an ask to another Identity Agent's AI agent the person's reach is not what governs it. The AI agent's own grant, which names the contact, and the owner's rule for that contact are the reach. The person must still stand: whatever keeps the AI agents checks, before it calls `CoreServer.SendAgentPeerMessage`, on every ask to another Identity Agent as on every other, that a person who opened the conversation still stands (ADR-047), so a conversation a removed person opened reaches no further. An AI agent that opened a chain must itself hold a grant naming the contact.
2. *A conversation is opened by a person's message, or by an AI agent at rest toward an AI agent its grant names (Amendment 3).* A conversation is also opened, on the receiving side, by an ask from an AI agent another Identity Agent holds, at depth 1, under the owner's rule for that contact.

## Consequences

- Two envelope types are registered (`agent-peer-message`, `agent-peer-directory`), and the receiving core has an action for each; neither hands anything on until an add-on registers a keeper (`CoreServer.OnAgentPeerMessage`, `CoreServer.OnAgentPeerDirectory`).
- In the core: the exchange message's route, content, signing and checks (`agentpeer`, over keri-go's `exchange.go`), the shared salted fingerprint (`fingerprint`), its vectors and contract, `agent.message.peer` and its grant check, the record's `peer_proof` and its check in the conversation bundle verifier, the sending methods (`CoreServer.SendAgentPeerMessage`, `CoreServer.SendAgentPeerDirectory`), and the directory's format (`agentpeer.Directory`: an Identity Agent tells a contact which of the AI agents it holds the contact may reach, by name, so the contact writes `name@alias`; it carries no signature and grants nothing, since what is reachable is each owner's rule).
- Not in the core: the owner's per-contact rule, its rate and its route, the conversations on each side, and naming. They belong to whatever keeps AI agents.
- Decided: the AI agent's own grant naming the contact and the owner's rule are the governance of a message to another Identity Agent's AI agent, with the person's standing re-checked (see above). An AI agent that opened a chain must itself hold a grant naming the contact.
- Decided: a message to a contact that is not answering is refused at once, and the entry that was written for it is marked not delivered. Keeping it and trying again until a time (ADR-069) needs the words at rest and comes later.
- The exchange message's time bound is an hour, the envelope's own. A message queued longer is made again when it is sent again.
- A name an Identity Agent gives the AI agents it holds to a contact is the contact's word. An alias that could be read as another name (`@`, `~`) is not used, and a name that matches more than one AI agent is refused rather than resolved to the first.
